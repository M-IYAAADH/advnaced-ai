## 3. Recommendations

### RQ1: land fraction and edge cells
- Literature support for a numeric cutoff: none found. No flood-susceptibility study in my searches states a land-fraction rule. The only on-point precedent is an analogue (Budic et al.): weight partial-land cells by land proportion rather than drop them, and compute covariates over the land part only. Their 10% value concerns species range overlap, not a land cutoff.
- So any cutoff (0.1, 0.25, 0.5) is a project choice and must be labelled as such, not as literature-derived.
- Suggested plan for MF-26: (a) compute covariates over the land part of each cell only (mask the sea), as Budic et al. did; (b) run the primary analysis with a threshold sensitivity at land_fraction >= 0.10, 0.25 and 0.50, reporting how many cells (of 75) and how many of the about 11 landmark-bearing cells remain at each; (c) a weight-by-land-fraction variant as a fourth run; (d) treat dropped cells as "not assessed", not low susceptibility. Check first that no landmark-report cell falls under the cutoff; if one does, keep it or merge it into its neighbour and say so.
- Limits: the 19 cells under 0.25 land are mostly coast and jetties, where DEM and land-cover values are unreliable; this reasoning is mine, not from a source. Merging with a neighbour has no source either.

### RQ2: spatial blocks with about 11 positives in 75 cells
- Sources agree that random CV overstates skill under spatial structure (Roberts abstract; Ploton full text: random R2 0.53 against spatial R2 0.14). Block size should be near or above the autocorrelation range (Ploton: block "slightly longer" than the range; blockCV provides range estimation). Ploton also shows that even that can be too small.
- Island is about 1.9 km across a few cells; a 200 m grid with a plausible range of several hundred metres leaves very few independent blocks. Four wards fit too coarsely (one ward may hold most positives; some folds would have zero positives), whereas groups of neighbouring cells fit better. Suggest 5 to 6 contiguous blocks of about 12 to 15 cells (a 3 by 3 or 4 by 4 cell block, roughly 600 to 800 m), built so each block holds at least 1 to 2 positives, where a block is a hard unit: all records and any duplicates in a cell, and neighbouring cells within the block, stay in one fold. This is my judgement; no source gives a fold count for such small data.
- Estimate the autocorrelation range from the covariates (blockCV approach) or from the positives with a variogram or Moran's I, but with 75 cells the estimate will be very uncertain; report it as indicative.
- Alternatives: leave-one-block-out with buffer (blockCV buffering; the buffer width unknown from my sources), or environmental blocking if spatial blocks cannot keep positives per fold. NNDM/kNNDM (Milà; Linnenbrink) is designed for clustered samples but needs prediction targets; here the prediction area is every cell, so it is applicable in principle, but I did not open details and with 11 positives it is likely unstable.
- Report fold-wise metrics and a pooled one, and keep the claim to "ranking positives above unreported cells". Unreported cells are unknown, not negatives. Spatial CV does not fix that.
- Where sources disagree: Wadoux et al. (2021) argue spatial CV is not the right map-accuracy measure (via IDEAS summary, unopened); Roberts warns blocking can induce extrapolation. Nothing found applies directly to 75 cells.
