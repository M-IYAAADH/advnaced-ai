# Location-specific rain-flood evidence for Malé island proper (as of 15 Nov 2026)

Scope note: Malé island proper only. Evidence types are labelled **[observed-photo]**, **[observed-news]**, **[agency statement]**, **[planning document]**, **[pumping/intervention site]** (where pumps or drains were placed: implies a problem location, not a measured flood extent), or **[model/projection]**. Swell/tidal events are listed separately. All PDFs marked "verified downloadable" were downloaded and their text extracted in this session (15 Nov 2026); news pages were fetched and read through a summarising fetch tool, so quoted wording should be rechecked against the page before citation.

## Q1. JICA survey/report on Malé (flood photos, drainage findings, elevation)

### Takeaway
The "JICA 2021" source is the *Data Collection Survey on Climate-Related Disasters in the Malé Region, Republic of Maldives: Final Report* (fieldwork Dec 2021 to Jan 2022, text written "as of March 2022"). It is verified downloadable and shows photographs of **Majeedhee Magu flooded after the rain of 11 Dec 2021 at two points: "near Henveiru Ground" and "Central Part"**. It also says the west and south-west of the island are lower and that the team's inland-flood walkover concentrated on the south-west. It contains no flood map and no street inventory.

### Cited Findings
- Exact running title: "Data Collection Survey on Climate-Related Disasters in the Malé Region Republic of Maldives, Final Report"; 112-page PDF, publisher JICA (Open JICA Report library). The consultant firm is not readable in the text layer because the cover is an image. Survey team: J. Yokokura (leader), S. Orishimo (coastal), Y. Takamura (flood and drainage; in country 2 Dec 2021 to 3 Jan 2022), M. Kanno. A footnote says "As of the writing of this report (March 2022)". So cite it as 2022, not 2021. Verified downloadable — [JICA PDF](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12368783.pdf)
- Front-matter photos (p. iv) and Figure 3-2.4 (report p. 52): "Road condition after rainfall on December 11, 2021 (Near Henveiru Ground in Majeedhee Magu Rd., Male')" and "(Central Part in Majeedhee Magu Rd., Male')". Source: JICA Survey Team. Figure 2-1.2 (p. 14) is captioned "Inland flooding in Male' City, 11 December 2021" with no street given. Evidence type: [observed-photo]; bounded only to a landmark (Henveiru Ground) and an unspecified central stretch of Majeedhee Magu — [JICA PDF](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12368783.pdf)
- Rainfall stated: the airport-island gauge recorded 133 mm/day on 11 Dec 2021, with three peaks of 31–33 mm/hour, and 45 mm/hour in the early morning of 12 Dec. The report calls this the second-heaviest in 30 years. (The gauge is on Hulhulé, not Malé proper.) — [JICA PDF](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12368783.pdf), p. 50
- Elevation: Malé and Hulhumalé are around 1–2 m above mean sea level. MLSA reported that **no ground-level data** existed and that neither MLSA nor MNPHI planned a topographic map. "The topography of Male Island does not have depressions… but the elevation of the west side and the southwest side are lower than the east side, so there is a strong need for inundation measures on the western side of the island." — [JICA PDF](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12368783.pdf), p. 49
- Method: "Visual survey on foot and photography of inland inundation… In Male' Island, the survey activities were conducted mainly in the southwest area where inland water frequently occurs." — [JICA PDF](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12368783.pdf), Ch. 1-3
- Drainage findings (p. 51–52): "the exact damaged areas and the situations are unknown because the monitoring regime has not been established". The report lists four causes of temporary road floods: (1) concrete-block roads with non-uniform slope; (2) narrow roads without side drains; (3) insufficient drainage-pump capacity, with sewage illegally connected to drainage pipes; (4) blocked outlets and side drains from poor maintenance. According to MNPHI, road flooding resolves within 3 hours. MCC (road drains) and MWSC (pumps and pipes) development was "not consistent". — [JICA PDF](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12368783.pdf)
- MCC told the survey that MLSA's "DEM" is in fact a DSM (it includes buildings and trees) and "cannot be used for planning drainage systems". — [JICA PDF](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12368783.pdf), Ch. 2
- Context: a Streetscaping Project was rebuilding 13 streets (1.97 km) in Henveiru from April 2021 and 8 streets (1.9 km) in Maafannu as of Dec 2021 (names not given in the extracted text). A Japanese-funded "Project for Disaster Resilience Enhancement in Malé" (seawall plus stormwater drainage equipment) was later signed. Its construction-phase grant was signed 9 Sep 2025 — [JICA press release](https://www.jica.go.jp/english/information/press/2025/20250904_12.html); [MoF report](https://mfr.mv/public-sector/government-of-maldives-and-jica-have-signed-an-agreement-for-the-project-for-disaster-resilience-enhancement-in-male)
- Licence/access: free public PDF from JICA's Open JICA Report library. No open licence is stated in the extracted text, so use it with citation and do not redistribute.

### Inferences
- The JICA photos give two observed, dated (11 Dec 2021) road-section points on Majeedhee Magu. Code them as landmark-bounded sections ("near Henveiru Ground", which is the east end of Majeedhee Magu, plus an unspecified central section), not as the whole road.
- The "west/southwest lower" statement is a qualitative expert observation, not a survey measurement. The report itself says no ground-level data existed.

### Gaps
- Exact catalogue/publication date and consultant name were not confirmed because the cover is an image. Check the Open JICA Report catalogue entry.
- The preparatory survey report for the 2024–25 Disaster Resilience Enhancement project (it may contain pump-station catchments and flood hotspots) was not found online.
- Whether there are further unlabelled south-west photos in appendices was not checked page-by-page.

## Q2. Peer-reviewed / grey literature mapping flood-prone streets or areas

### Takeaway
I found **no peer-reviewed paper or thesis that maps flood-prone streets of Malé**. The best area-level grey-literature evidence is: (a) the World Bank-funded Stormwater Masterplan ToR, which identifies the **south and south-west reclaimed land** with typical flood depths of about 0.40 m; (b) the Malé City Council VLR 2025, which names **Galolhu and Henveiru wards** for "frequent drainage failures"; (c) JICA (above) on the west/south-west. A Research Square preprint gives IDF curves for Malé but no locations.

### Cited Findings
- **Ministry of Finance, "Terms of Reference: Consultancy for Development of Stormwater Management Masterplans for Male' City and Hulhumale'", Maldives Urban Development and Resilience Project (World Bank)**. Undated; it references the Dec 2018 flood and end-2017 figures, so it was probably issued around 2019. Verified downloadable (8 pages) — [MoF PDF](https://www.finance.gov.mv/public/attachments/s8qW2r7m2YxQyTjcqNU2TWJtRtHFhiOymOHc1nTY.pdf)
  - "the reclaimed lands in the south and south-west in particular have been suffering from flooding, as stormwater from the higher, older city area comes down… Flooding depths are generally in the order of about 0.40 meter… the most recent one was experienced in December 2018." [agency/planning statement; depth is approximate and not tied to a location]
  - The old city (about 1 km²) sits on the highest ground, at 1–2 m. The reclaimed land, "with an elevation lower than the natural land c.q. the old city, accumulates all the flood waters and gets heavily flooded even with relatively light intensity rains."
  - Drainage: about 60 km of paved roads (about 57 km interlocking blocks, about 3 km asphalt). Gutters along all paved roads feed cross-pipes to soak pits every 6 m. 15 pumping stations were installed 2014–2018 in the low-lying reclaimed areas. The annex listing them was **not present in the extracted text**.
  - Task 4 asks the consultant to assess "flooding hotspots, locations, depth and duration of flooding". If the masterplan was delivered, it would be the key hotspot dataset, but it is not public.
- **Malé City Council (2025), *Voluntary Local Review Report of Malé City Council, Maldives 2025: Moving towards a Safe, Inclusive, Equitable, Resilient and Sustainable Island City*.** Published Sept 2025, with technical support from ICLEI South Asia and UNESCAP. © Malé City Council; reproduction only with permission. Verified downloadable (144 pp) — [MCC PDF](https://cdn.malecity.gov.mv/website/documents/6zqnEssfa8YfgaNZkf_I0-VLR_Male_Small%20file%20size.pdf)
  - p. 92: "Surface Water Flooding: 8 major events (2019–2024), including: 31 Dec 2023: 179.7 mm rainfall (highest in 26 years); 12 Jan 2024: 143.4 mm rainfall… Over 282 households… affected in a single 2024 event; Frequent drainage failures in Galolhu and Henveiru wards." [agency statement, ward-level; rainfall station and accumulation period not stated]
  - p. 93, coastal: "5 August 2023: Rogue wave inundated roads near the Sinamalé Bridge" and "Overtopping at Industrial Village during southwest monsoon". [agency statement, swell]
  - The MCC Disaster Management Committee (formed Jan 2025) deployed more than 4,500 sandbags "in flood-prone areas". The locations are not named.
  - "95% of its land area is at risk of flooding by 2100 (IPCC projections)". This is a **projection**, not observed evidence, and it is not street-specific. It is also reported by [Plus.mv, 14 Jan 2026](https://www.plus.mv/?p=154072).
- **Research Square preprint, "Rainfall Trend and Intensity-Duration-Frequency (IDF) Analysis for Malé, Maldives (1993-2024)"**. The fetched page showed only the title. The search snippet reports a 100-year 24-h design depth of 216.9 mm and hourly data from 1993–2024. It is not peer-reviewed, and it has **no street-level locations** — [Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10674253/v1)
- Maldives National University repository (Saruna) search surfaced only non-Malé flood EIAs (e.g. Feydhoo, Addu) — [Saruna item](https://saruna.mnu.edu.mv/items/13dff806-1092-4c80-99d1-40507cd5239a). No Malé street-level flood thesis was found.

### Inferences
- The combined grey literature supports an area-level prior: lower south and south-west reclaimed land (which includes much of Maafannu and parts of Galolhu/Machangolhi south) is wetter than the higher old city. Ward names in the VLR (Galolhu, Henveiru) and news (Maafannu) partly conflict with a purely south-west pattern. Henveiru's Majeedhee Magu east end also appears in JICA's photos. Treat these as qualitative and hypothesis-generating.
- Mapping the ward names onto the ToR's "south/south-west reclaimed" description is my inference. The ToR names no wards.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed article, thesis or flood-hazard map naming Malé streets was found in the searches run (Google-type web search, Saruna). Google Scholar, Villa College and overseas theses were not searched exhaustively.
- The ToR pump-station annex, the masterplan deliverables and the MCC "survey identifying flood-prone areas for priority repairs" ([Atoll Times, 28 Dec 2024](https://atolltimes.mv/post/news/10872)) were not found publicly.

## Q3. Copernicus EMS, UNOSAT, Sentinel Asia or International Charter activations

### Takeaway
No activation for flooding in Malé or the Maldives was found. This is expected, because a city of about 2 km² with short-lived road ponding is below the scale these services map.

### Cited Findings
- A web search for Copernicus EMS, Sentinel Asia or Charter activations for Maldives/Malé flooding returned only other countries' activations (Thailand, Sumatra, Sri Lanka) — e.g. [Charter activation, Thailand flood](https://disasterscharter.org/fr/activations/flood-in-thailand-activation-1004-); [Copernicus EMS news, Sri Lanka](https://mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu/news/surface-deformation-analysis-following-tropical-cyclone-ditwah-25-in-sri-lanka-emsn230/)

### Inferences
- Satellite rapid-mapping products are unlikely to exist. Even if they did, 10 m-class SAR cannot resolve narrow street flooding between dense buildings.

### Gaps
- The activation lists themselves (Copernicus EMS viewer, Charter activations page, Sentinel Asia EOS list, UNOSAT map catalogue) were not filtered by country directly. A negative from search is not fully verified.

## Q4. Crowd-sourced / photo / news evidence naming streets (by event)

### Takeaway
News outlets name only a few places, and they repeat: **Majeedhee Magu, Ameenee Magu, Boduthakurufaanu Magu, Izzuddeen Magu (Maafannu), the West Park area, near IGMH, Villimalé ferry terminal area, Boduvalhu, the STO flour warehouse area, the Carnival area**. Most of the event reports checked (13 Aug 2024, 3–4 Jan 2025, 10–11 Jan 2025) name **no streets** at all ("various parts of the city"). This shows reporting bias towards main roads and pump sites.

### Cited Findings (rain, observed or agency-reported; newest first)
- **~30 Jul 2026 (stated as "Thursday")**: temporary flooding after heavy rain on the newly built four-lane **Boduthakurufaanu Magu section from Henveiru Ferry Terminal to Izzuddin Jetty**. The cause given is that the permanent pump station had not yet been commissioned, and MNDF used portable pumps. [observed-news; bounded section] — [The Standard, "Fact Check: Boduthakurufaanu Magu flooding was not caused by road defects or a design flaw" (Ahmed Ashraf)](https://standard.mv/fact-check-boduthakurufaanu-magu-flooding-was-not-caused-by-road-defects-or-a-design-flaw/)
- **11 Jan 2025** (rain overnight 10–11 Jan): MNDF deployed pumps in 9 areas. The article names only **West Park area (5 pumps), vicinity of IGMH (2), Boduvalhu area (1)**. [pumping/intervention site] — [The Standard, "Heavy Rainfall Causes Severe Flooding in Malé: MNDF Mobilizes to Drain Malé Streets", 11 Jan 2025](https://standard.mv/heavy-rainfall-causes-severe-flooding-in-male-mndf-mobilizes-to-drain-male-streets/)
- **3–4 Jan 2025**: 17 pumps were deployed "in different areas" and more than 30,000 tonnes of water were drained (11:37 Fri to 08:30 Sat). **No locations named** — [Atoll Times, "Over 30,000 tonnes of water drained from Male", 4 Jan 2025](https://atolltimes.mv/post/news/10922). A search snippet cites 161.9 mm on 3 Jan 2025 (source and station not verified).
- **28 Dec 2024**: MCC closed **Majeedhee Magu between Dhanburuh Magu and Nikagas Magu** for flood-mitigation works. It reported repairs done on **Chaandhanee Magu and Rahdhebai Magu**, and said **areas near IGMH and the STO flour warehouse** "experience severe flooding during rains". An MCC survey identified the flood-prone areas. [agency statement; bounded section + landmarks] — [Atoll Times, "Part of Majeedhee Magu closed for flood mitigation work", 28 Dec 2024](https://atolltimes.mv/post/news/10872). Edition.mv describes the stretch as one that "floods the most during the rainy season" — [Edition, "Parts of Majeedhee Magu to be closed to fix flooding issues"](https://edition.mv/ahmed_usham_attorny_general/38397) (search snippet only; not fetched)
- **18 Nov 2024**: "Nearly all streets, including smaller lanes" flooded. Named: **Majeedhee Magu, Ameenee Magu**. No rainfall amount. [observed-news; whole-road] — [Edition, "Streets of Male' flooded in torrential downpour…", 18 Nov 2024](https://edition.mv/un/37549)
- **13 Oct 2024**: temporary drainage systems installed in flood-prone areas at **West Park, Villimalé Ferry Terminal (on Malé), near IGMH**. The ministry was working in "five key areas", but only these three are named. [intervention site] — [Plus.mv, "Temporary Measures to Mitigate Flooding in Malé Near Completion", 13 Oct 2024](https://www.plus.mv/?p=104849)
- **13 Aug 2024**: flooding in "some areas of the city". **No locations named**, and no Malé rainfall total is given (231.5 mm is quoted for HA. Kelaa, not Malé) — [Atoll Times, "Male flooded; soldiers drain roads, help people", 13 Aug 2024](https://atolltimes.mv/post/news/9494)
- **19 Jun 2024**: MCC began fixing flood-prone roads, starting with **Izzuddeen Magu, Maafannu** ("slow to drain when it rains"; Falaah Pre-School). Infrastructure Minister Muththalib said the **West Park area** is "most impacted by flooding" because of how its roads are built. [agency statement] — [Edition, "Malé City Council to fix flood prone roads", 19 Jun 2024](https://edition.mv/jakarta/34122)
- **Mar 2025**: the council deployed two pumps "mainly… in the Maafannu district, which is the most flood-prone area in Malé City", with Henveiru planned next. This is a search snippet and was not fetched — [Raajje.mv](https://raajje.mv/164166)
- **31 Dec 2023 / 12 Jan 2024**: the VLR gives 179.7 mm and 143.4 mm and names wards (Galolhu, Henveiru), but no streets — [MCC VLR 2025](https://cdn.malecity.gov.mv/website/documents/6zqnEssfa8YfgaNZkf_I0-VLR_Male_Small%20file%20size.pdf)
- **11 Dec 2021**: JICA photos of Majeedhee Magu (see Q1) — [JICA PDF](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12368783.pdf)
- **Dec 2018**: the ToR cites the south/south-west reclaimed areas — [MoF ToR](https://www.finance.gov.mv/public/attachments/s8qW2r7m2YxQyTjcqNU2TWJtRtHFhiOymOHc1nTY.pdf)
- **17 Aug 2015** (historic): **Maafannu ward** (some streets about 1 ft of water; "lower-lying… prone to frequent floods"), **Ameenee Magu** (impassable for a time), **IGMH** (roof leaks), **Carnival area**. Rain lasted "just over two hours", with no mm amount — [Maldives Independent, "Malé under water: frequent floods demonstrate climate vulnerability", 17 Aug 2015](https://maldivesindependent.com/feature-comment/male-under-water-frequent-floods-demonstrate-climate-vulnerability-116499)
- Undated Sun-type report (seen only in a search snippet): **Ameenee Magu, Boduthakurufaanu Magu, Majeedhee Magu** heavily flooded on a Sunday morning. The date is unknown, so it should not be used until the date is confirmed.
- Edition photo gallery (unverified content): [edition.mv/gallery/2956](https://edition.mv/gallery/2956). It surfaced in search but its captions and date were not checked.

### Swell / tidal (keep separate from rain)
- **26 Jul 2026**: tidal surges flooded the **Carnival area** and **the newly completed section of Boduthakurufaanu Magu**, and MNDF pumped them — [Atoll Times, "Tidal surges flood parts of Malé as MNDF begins water pumping", 26 Jul 2026](https://atolltimes.mv/post/news/16641). Note that this is the same BTM section reported flooded by rain about 4 days later. Attribute mechanism per event, not per road.
- **5 Aug 2023**: rogue wave inundated roads near **Sinamalé Bridge**. **Industrial Village** overtopping in the SW monsoon — [MCC VLR 2025](https://cdn.malecity.gov.mv/website/documents/6zqnEssfa8YfgaNZkf_I0-VLR_Male_Small%20file%20size.pdf), p. 93
- 2015, days before 17 Aug: tidal swells in the Carnival area — [Maldives Independent](https://maldivesindependent.com/feature-comment/male-under-water-frequent-floods-demonstrate-climate-vulnerability-116499)

### Licence/access
All the news articles are free to read and copyrighted by their outlets. Record facts and URLs and do not copy photos. The JICA and MoF PDFs are public. The VLR requires MCC permission to reproduce.

### Inferences
- Candidate positive locations with ≥2 independent sources: **Majeedhee Magu** (JICA 2021 photo; Edition 2024; MCC 2024 works on the Dhanburuh–Nikagas section), **Ameenee Magu** (2015, 2024), **West Park area** (minister 2024, temporary drain 2024, MNDF pumps 2025), **near IGMH** (2015, 2024 MCC, 2024 drain, 2025 pumps), **Maafannu ward / Izzuddeen Magu** (2015, 2024, 2025). **Boduthakurufaanu Magu new section** is mixed rain and tide (2026).
- Pump and temporary-drain sites indicate where agencies expect ponding. They are not measured extents.

### Gaps
- **Mapillary/KartaView, OSM flood tags, X/Facebook photo collections, and Sun, Avas, PSM and Mihaaru (Dhivehi) galleries were not searched** within this tool budget. Dhivehi-language reports (Mihaaru, Avas) are likely to name more streets.
- Dates 30–31 Dec 2023, 10–12 Jan 2024, 1 Mar 2025 and most 2026 events were not traced to street-naming articles beyond the VLR's dates and rainfall.
- MNDF's tweets listing all 9 (Jan 2025) or 17 (Jan 2025) pump locations were not retrieved. They would be the richest open location list.

## Q5. Malé City Council / government statements naming flood-prone roads or low-lying areas

### Takeaway
There is **no published official list** of flood-prone roads. Named items come from scattered statements: MCC (Izzuddeen Magu; the Majeedhee Magu Dhanburuh–Nikagas section; Chaandhanee and Rahdhebai Magu repairs; IGMH and STO flour-warehouse areas; Galolhu and Henveiru drainage failures; Maafannu as most flood-prone), the infrastructure ministry (West Park; temporary drains at West Park, Villimalé ferry terminal and IGMH) and the MoF ToR (south/south-west reclaimed land).

### Cited Findings
- MCC repairs and survey of flood-prone areas: [Atoll Times, 28 Dec 2024](https://atolltimes.mv/post/news/10872); [Edition, 19 Jun 2024](https://edition.mv/jakarta/34122)
- Ministry temporary drainage sites: [Plus.mv, 13 Oct 2024](https://www.plus.mv/?p=104849)
- MCC VLR ward statement: [MCC VLR 2025, p. 92](https://cdn.malecity.gov.mv/website/documents/6zqnEssfa8YfgaNZkf_I0-VLR_Male_Small%20file%20size.pdf)
- In Mar 2026 the mayor claimed more than 400 roads repaired and more than 40,000 drainage canals cleared across Malé and Vilimalé, but named no roads — [Atoll Times, 16 Mar 2026](https://atolltimes.mv/post/news/15234)

### Inferences
- Repaired roads (Chaandhanee, Rahdhebai, Izzuddeen) are "agency-identified as flood-prone before repair". After repair their status is unknown, which matters for any time-varying labels.

### Gaps
- The MCC flood-prone-area survey (2024), the MCC sandbag deployment list (2025) and the MWSC list of 15–16 pump stations are not public. Request them from MCC, MWSC and NDMA.
