# Malé reported-flood analysis: methods and sensitivity

This is exploratory agreement with a positive-only, secondary report inventory. Unreported dates and roads are **unknown**, not observations of dry conditions. It does not estimate street susceptibility, flood depths, physical flood probabilities, or warning performance. Earlier provisional outputs in `derived/daily_exploration/` are retained; the extension is in `derived/extended_analysis/`.

## Labels, independent units and uncertainty

The compilation contains 18 incident records and 22 flagged calendar days; neither is a count of independently observed storms. The source review in `derived/daily_exploration/event_review.csv` retains 11 reported dates from 11 incidents, masks 14 uncertain dates, and excludes seven uncertain incident records. These are source-checked reports, not a complete flood census. Some reports only establish an approximate location or date; the source review documents the individual decisions and links. The previous 17–18 storm estimate for all 18 records is a hypothesis, not verified meteorology.

For conservative grouping, sort all compiled incident anchors, including uncertain records, and merge consecutive records separated by at most two calendar days, transitively. This groups 10 and 12 January 2024 together. The resulting IDs and decisions are in `incident_storm_groups.csv`; groups are **proxies**, not independently verified storms. Splits are chronological with an eight-day embargo, and group membership is checked at boundaries. The longest rainfall lookback is the feature day plus three prior days; lagging adds one further day. The eight-day gap is therefore wider than the used windows. Earlier provisional code used a wider seven-day feature requirement in its explanation, although its actual candidates used three days. No group may be shared across training and test.

## Time and source metadata

The source files have no original Open-Meteo request, timezone, or UTC offset. `time_local` is an undocumented compiler label; Maldives UTC+5 is plausible but not proven. [Open-Meteo's historical API documentation](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api) identifies hourly precipitation as the **preceding-hour sum** and explains that the requested timezone governs the response offset and daily boundaries. It cannot establish this particular downloaded file's requested timezone. Rainfall is summed by nominal timestamp day for the primary analysis, consistently with the supplied daily values, not asserted to be a verified local civil-day accumulation. Sea-level timestamp provenance is similarly unresolved.

[NOAA GSOD metadata](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/metadata/landing-page/bin/iso?id=gov.noaa.ncdc%3AC00516&view=iso) states daily summaries can include part of the preceding day and are normally available 1–2 days later. GSOD rainfall accumulation flags and station-specific windows still need verification; the Hulhulé station is outside Malé and is not used as a feature in these experiments. Observed UHSLC 2024 and modelled sea level have different datums and are not pooled. Historical availability of reanalysis is not established, so the lagged view is not an operational forecast.

Pre-specified alternatives are nominal timestamps, rainfall timestamps shifted by −1 or +1 hour before daily aggregation, and all reviewed positive and uncertain label dates shifted together by −1 or +1 day. These are stress tests, not claims that either offset is correct; shifting the timestamp does not resolve preceding-hour boundary semantics. No alignment was selected from performance. Results for every fold and alternative are in `metrics.csv` and `folds.csv`. For nominal rainfall-only logistic models, retrospective average precision across 2024–2026 is 0.309, 0.389 and 0.243; lagged is 0.015, 0.156 and 0.010. Under the one-day label alternatives, retrospective values range 0.010–0.574 across folds, and lagged values range 0.005–0.611. These ranges also include differences in training and test event assignment; they are sensitivity illustrations, not confidence intervals. Scores respond strongly to date definitions and tiny event counts.

## Why the earlier result used 222 days

The original script started eligibility in 2023 and required every candidate field to be present, including optional station and sea-level data. Station data end on 24 August 2025, so its planned 2025 test window had 236 calendar days. In the same-day analysis, four dates were masked as uncertain (2 and 4 January; 11 and 12 March) and ten days had no daily sea-level maximum (30 January–8 February), leaving **222**. The exclusions do not overlap. Its 2024 test was skipped because its training period, beginning 2023 and ending 23 December 2023 after the embargo, had only one retained positive; the rule required two. The only subsequent planned period ended at the station endpoint, leaving one usable test period. The lagged eligibility calculation shifts features and also yields 222, but its missing feature dates shift by a day. These are code-defined restrictions, not proof that other rainfall-only periods are unavailable.

## Evaluation

Rainfall-only uses the full defensible hourly record, complete 24-hour nominal days, a three-day prior rainfall sum, and retained labels. Comparisons with sea level require all 24 hourly sea-level values and use precisely the same days for rainfall and rainfall-plus-sea models. Annual test folds are defined in advance; training uses all earlier eligible data through 23 December of the preceding year. Folds require at least two training storm groups and one test storm group. The 2023 fold fails because only one training storm is available for rainfall, and the 2024 sea-level comparison fails because only one training storm remains on that coverage. No additional annual folds satisfy the rule before 2024 for rainfall or before 2025 for sea level. There is no independent final holdout beyond these exploratory folds. See `folds.csv` for exact endpoints, exclusions, positive days and storm counts, including the lagged and sensitivity cases.

Prevalence is the training reported-label prevalence; seasonal logistic uses annual sine/cosine; the rainfall-threshold baseline chooses a rainfall threshold using training F1 over fixed training quantiles. Logistic hyperparameters and the 0.5 score cutoff are fixed in advance; scaling is fitted on each training fold only. No test data are used to select the threshold, model or alignment. Class weighting means scores are not calibrated probabilities. Precision, recall, and average precision measure **agreement with reported outcomes**; unreported flooding may be counted as disagreement. A storm group here contains one retained day, so storm detection is equivalent to positive-day recall in these folds, not a measure of event duration.

On identical sea-level-complete dates, retrospective rainfall vs rainfall-plus-sea AP is 0.409 vs 0.302 in 2025 (349 days, four groups) and 0.243 vs 0.208 in 2026 (315 days, two groups); lagged is 0.170 vs 0.193 and 0.010 vs 0.010 respectively. This is too little evidence to infer the value of sea level. Full baseline precision, recall, AP, flagged days and sample sizes are in `metrics.csv` and the dashboard. AP and any apparent precision depend on the reporting process, not verified dry days.

## Source inventory and remaining validation

The detailed source and field inventory, missingness, licences, and provenance are in `reports/data_inventory_and_progress_report.md` and starter-pack READMEs. The 2024–2025 NOAA hourly groups have overlapping 6-, 12- and 24-hour accumulations and must not be summed together; UHSLC 2024 has 125 missing hourly observations. Complete-day requirements in the extension are stricter than the preliminary maximum-of-available-hours rule. Spatial locations in the dashboard are limited to source-checked flooding statements joined to approximate landmark points; they do not establish individual road segments, and different reports at the same landmark may overlap visually.
