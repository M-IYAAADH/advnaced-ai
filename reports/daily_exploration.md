# Preliminary daily reported-flood experiment

**Update:** The later [extended analysis](extended_analysis.md) adds separate coverage, sensitivity tests, figures and a dashboard. The findings and outstanding-work list below describe this preliminary run.

This is an incomplete exploratory analysis, not a flood-warning system or a street-level model. The target is agreement with reports of rainwater flooding on Malé island proper. A zero means **no report in the present inventory**, not a confirmed non-flood day. Apparent false positives may be unreported flooding. The review is not an exhaustive search for all events.

## Event review

`derived/daily_exploration/event_review.csv` reviews all 18 compiled incidents against source evidence and retains 11 dated reported events; seven incidents are uncertain and 14 dates in all are masked. A retained event may have additional uncertain days. The 10 and 12 January 2024 records may be the same storm; the latter is excluded pending verification. The original JICA 2021 date is not verified. The article about 1 April 2023 contains conflicting publication and relative timing. The 20–21 December 2024 report supports flooding on the preceding weekend but does not uniquely identify the day. The MMTV article supports flooding in the March 2025 spell but does not uniquely establish a date. A source can verify citywide flooding without verifying every individual pump location. Geographic claims in the retained events identify Malé proper, while airport and other islands are not outcomes. The 2022 event relies on the earlier documented source check in `verification/landmark_checks.csv`; the source could not be opened in this session.

No claim of 11 statistically independent storms is warranted until close-together records and date uncertainties are reviewed further. Two of the included incidents have proximity/relative date uncertainties explained in the review. The verification table retains URLs and uncertainty rather than treating pumping alone as proof of flooding.

## Data and features

See `reports/data_inventory_and_progress_report.md` for the detailed source inventory and coverage. Reanalysis hourly and daily CSVs are compiler-described ERA5 via Open-Meteo, in mm, hours and meteorological units. Airport station GSOD is observed on nearby Hulhulé, not Malé; daily rain is mm and can be missing. Modelled marine sea level is in metres relative to mean sea level. The separate UHSLC tide gauge is observed UTC hourly in mm relative to station zero; it is not combined with the modelled level. No original Open-Meteo request metadata establishes the `time_local` offset or daily boundary. [Open-Meteo documentation](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api) states hourly precipitation is the preceding-hour sum and that the requested timezone sets daily boundaries; neither resolves this particular download's unknown request. GSOD accumulation flags describe different accumulation windows, not automatic calendar days; the station has known date discrepancies, so it is omitted as a next-day predictor. The reanalysis and modelled sea-level date boundaries and their historic availability also remain unresolved; next-day scores are retrospective lagged experiments, not demonstrated operational forecasts.

`daily_features.csv` is rebuilt directly from source inputs, without the supplied prelabelled table. Previous 1/3/7-day rain sums exclude the feature day and require complete windows. Missing rainfall stays missing. The 1-hour maximum is maximum hourly reanalysis precipitation as timestamped. Daily rain and wet hours use the supplied daily file. Sea level is the maximum modelled level within the file's nominal timestamp day. The next-day experiment shifts all feature columns by one row before joining to the target day. The source overlap restriction requires all compared source features to be present, so no missing rainfall is imputed into the analysis sample; pipelines nonetheless fit an imputer and scaler on training data only. The flood identifier is retained for audit and never passed to the model.

## Evaluation and provisional findings

One usable common-source chronological test period remains: 1 January–24 August 2025, 222 eligible days and three reported events; training through 23 December 2024 contains 649 eligible days and four reported events for the retrospective experiment, three for the shifted experiment. The eight-day boundary gap exceeds the seven-day antecedent window including the extra next-day shift. Earlier planned test folds had too few training or test positives and were skipped. No separate final holdout is possible from these common-source data. For these one-day retained events, event detection means a positive prediction on the retained reported day, so event-level detection equals recall; this does not resolve multi-day event detection.

The test reported-label prevalence was 1.35%. At a fixed 0.5 classification cutoff, the retrospective rainfall-only logistic model had average precision 0.667, precision 0.158 (3 of 19 flagged days) and recall 1.0 (3 of 3 reported days). The lagged rainfall-only model had average precision 0.241, precision 0.070 (3 of 43) and recall 1.0 (3 of 3). These values are extremely unstable, conditional on a selectively reported inventory and unknown time alignment, and are not physical flood probabilities or evidence of warning performance. The small sample cannot support reliable ranking of feature sets or confidence intervals. A threshold chosen using training F1 alone is a descriptive baseline, not a flooding threshold. Complete results and confusion counts are in `metrics.csv`; saved scores are held-out only.

## Reproduce

From the project root, in an environment with Python 3.12, install `numpy==2.4.4 pandas==3.0.2 scikit-learn==1.8.0 matplotlib==3.10.9`, then run `MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/male-matplotlib LOKY_MAX_CPU_COUNT=2 python3 scripts/explore_daily_flood.py`. The script reads raw inputs and the manually reviewed event table, writes only to `derived/daily_exploration/`, saves fold-fitted pipelines with pickle, and checks coverage, missingness, feature lag and event overlap. Only load pickle files that you trust and in a compatible Python environment.
