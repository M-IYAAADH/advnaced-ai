# Public sources can rank Malé roads, not label them

*Research synthesis, checked as of 15 Nov 2026. Scope: Malé island proper only. Hulhumalé, Vilimalé and other Greater Malé sources appear only as context.*

No public dataset lists flooded and dry roads on Malé island proper, and no public terrain source is finer than the 30 m FABDEM the project already holds. The open record still supports a defensible **positive-only** road-flood inventory and an area-level prior. A 2022 JICA survey report photographs Majeedhee Magu flooded at two points on 11 Dec 2021. A World Bank-era terms of reference says the **lower south and south-west reclaimed land** floods to about **0.40 m**. Malé City Council's 2025 Voluntary Local Review names **Galolhu and Henveiru** wards for frequent drainage failures. News and agency statements from 2015 to 2026 repeatedly name Majeedhee Magu, Ameenee Magu, Izzuddeen Magu, a bounded section of Boduthakurufaanu Magu, and the areas near West Park and IGMH. The detailed data that would let the team move from "reported" to "susceptible" all sits with agencies and is unpublished: the Malé stormwater design from World Bank contract MV-MONPI-234588, any road survey levels, the list of 15–16 pump stations, and a 2024 Council survey of flood-prone areas. For rainfall, the open options are Ogimet SYNOP 6-, 12- and 24-hour totals from the Hulhulé station and half-hourly GPM IMERG at roughly 10 km resolution. Neither measures rain on Malé's streets. The team should build its susceptibility features from open layers and treat every news or pump-site mention as a positive report, never as a flooded/dry label. The one request most likely to change what the study can claim is for the stormwater design drawings and survey.

**How to read the status labels.** "Verified downloadable" means the researchers opened the file or service and read its contents on 14–15 Nov 2026. "Verified listed" means a catalogue or API returned the dataset entry but the data were not opened. "Mentioned only" means a document or search result refers to the item but no public file was found. Several news items were read through a summarising fetch tool, so recheck exact quotations against the original page before quoting them in the literature review.

## No open terrain source resolves Malé streets below 30 m

Elevation is the weakest input. Researchers queried OpenAerialMap, OpenTopography (including federated sources) and the Maxar Open Data catalogue for the Malé bounding box, and all three returned nothing ([OAM query](https://api.openaerialmap.org/meta?bbox=73.495,4.165,73.525,4.185&limit=50); [OpenTopography query](https://portal.opentopography.org/API/otCatalog?productFormat=PointCloud&minx=73.49&miny=4.16&maxx=73.53&maxy=4.19&detail=false&outputFormat=json&include_federated=true); [Maxar catalog](https://maxar-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/events/catalog.json)). The Housing Development Corporation's ArcGIS server does publish a **0.30 m DSM**, a point cloud and dated orthomosaics from 2024 to 2026, but converting their extents to WGS84 places them on **Hulhumalé** (about 73.53–73.55 E), not Malé (about 73.50–73.52 E). The server declares no licence and no vertical datum ([HDC services](https://gis.hdc.mv/server/rest/services?f=json); [Hulhumale_25_dsm](https://gis.hdc.mv/server/rest/services/Hulhumale_25_dsm/ImageServer?f=json)).

The government's own position, as reported in 2022, is that the data gap is real. The Maldives Land and Survey Authority told the JICA survey team that **no ground-level data existed** for Malé and that no topographic map was planned. Malé City Council added that the national "DEM" is actually a **DSM including buildings and trees** that "cannot be used for planning drainage systems". The same report gives the only expert description of Malé's terrain the team can cite: no depressions, with the **west and south-west lower than the east** ([JICA 2022, pp. 49 and Ch. 2](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12368783.pdf)). A World Bank-financed consultancy to build a Malé DEM was advertised in 2023, with the stated aim to "highlight which areas are susceptible to flooding" ([MoF EOI MV-MONPI-326937](https://www.finance.gov.mv/public/attachments/dAKJKgV8UvVvJxMwb0ilB8RlMvLEd7rCcKS4xePM.pdf)). The master-plan line it belonged to was dropped in 2024 ([ISR Seq. 10](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099110624015595404/txt/P1639571d696880c419009102f1f185416a.txt); [Audit FIN-2025-73(E)](https://www.audit.gov.mv/Uploads/AuditReports/2025/06June/Maldives_Urban_Development_and_Resilience_Project_2024___Financial_Statement_Audit.pdf)). CLAUDE.md gives a cancellation date of 12 Aug 2024. This research did not confirm that date from a primary source. The procurement plan's 2024-08-13 is a scheduled date, not a cancellation notice.

The 2022 LAMER topographic survey and the 2023 "Third Additional Information" EIA addendum for the Malé stormwater project were **not found**. The old EPA file links now redirect to `era.gov.mv` and return 404, and the new ERA reports API also returns 404, so the EIA library cannot be searched online ([ERA reports page](https://www.era.gov.mv/reports.html)). No public Royal HaskoningDHV study or "DEM_interpolateRHDHV" figure for Malé was traced either. The closest methodological precedent is Brown et al. (2020), who built a **10 m Hulhumalé DEM from 8,706 surveyed road and defence points** and ran LISFLOOD-FP ([J. Flood Risk Mgmt, 10.1111/jfr3.12567](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jfr3.12567)). That points to the real route for Malé: obtain road spot heights from whoever holds the stormwater design survey.

## The stormwater design exists on paper but not in public

The World Bank's Maldives Urban Development and Resilience Project (P163957) is the backbone of the drainage record. Its appraisal document describes a system **over forty years old**, with road gutters every **6 m** draining to soak pits, **15 pumping stations** built in 2014–2018 in the reclaimed areas, and average flood depths of about **0.4 m** on the reclaimed south and south-west. It names no streets ([PAD2923, 2020](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/753601583118045862/txt/Maldives-Urban-Development-Resilience-Project.txt)). The stormwater master-plan terms of reference adds that Malé has about **60 km of paved road**, nearly all interlocking blocks. It says the old city sits at 1–2 m and that reclaimed land "accumulates all the flood waters and gets heavily flooded even with relatively light intensity rains". It also asks the consultant to map "flooding hotspots, locations, depth and duration", which is exactly the dataset the team lacks. The pump-station annex is not in the public text ([MoF ToR](https://www.finance.gov.mv/public/attachments/s8qW2r7m2YxQyTjcqNU2TWJtRtHFhiOymOHc1nTY.pdf)).

The procurement record shows what was actually bought. Contract **MV-MONPI-234588-CS-CQS**, "Design and preparation of Tender Documents for Male' Stormwater Management", was **signed for US$319,999.40**. The US$5 million construction contract was still "Pending" in February 2024 ([Procurement plan, rev. 2024-02-13](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099021324111531535/pdf/P163957112c4000fd197021a54c74b17564.pdf)). The Auditor General then recorded that the **master plan (US$631,000) and civil works (US$5,167,831.12) were cancelled in 2024** after a "change in policy direction following the change in Administration" ([Audit FIN-2025-73(E)](https://www.audit.gov.mv/Uploads/AuditReports/2025/06June/Maldives_Urban_Development_and_Resilience_Project_2024___Financial_Statement_Audit.pdf)). Whether the design was delivered is genuinely unclear. ISR Sequence 10 says in prose that the "Storm Water Management detailed design are delivered", but its own indicator for detailed designs still reads **"No"** at 30 Oct 2024 ([ISR Seq. 10](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099110624015595404/txt/P1639571d696880c419009102f1f185416a.txt)). The project's own `Data_Request_Specification.md` says the designs were "reported completed May 2024". That statement was not located in any public document. Either way, the drawings (drain alignments, inlets, pump sumps, invert levels and the survey behind them) are held by the ministry's project management unit, not published.

Japan has since become the active drainage funder. A **JPY 1.56 billion** grant for the "Project for Disaster Resilience Enhancement in Malé Island" was signed on 9 Sep 2025 for seawall repair and stormwater equipment ([MFR.mv](https://mfr.mv/public-sector/government-of-maldives-and-jica-have-signed-an-agreement-for-the-project-for-disaster-resilience-enhancement-in-male)). A February 2026 package will automate **16 Malé pump stations** and add a monitoring station ([archive.mv](https://archive.mv/en/articles/4ZkW8A)). The discrepancy between 15 and 16 stations is unexplained. JICA grant aid normally rests on a preparatory survey, which could hold pump catchments and hotspots, but none was found on the Open JICA Report library.

## Location evidence is real but positive-only and arterial-biased

Every piece of location evidence found records where flooding *was* reported, where agencies *chose* to pump, or where they *chose* to repair. None records where water was observed absent. Five categories should be kept apart when coding: observed photos, observed news, agency statements, pump or intervention sites, and projections. Coastal or swell events form a separate mechanism.

The strongest single item is the JICA survey. Its team photographed **Majeedhee Magu "near Henveiru Ground" and at its "Central Part"** after the rain of 11 Dec 2021. The report records 133 mm that day at the Hulhulé airport gauge, with three peaks of 31–33 mm/h ([JICA 2022, p. 50–52](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12368783.pdf)). The report text is dated "as of March 2022", so cite it as 2022. Its walkover concentrated on the south-west "where inland water frequently occurs", which means its coverage is itself biased. It also gives four causes of road ponding that can become explainable features: non-uniform slope on concrete-block roads, narrow roads without side drains, undersized pumps with illegal sewage connections, and blocked outlets. The team can proxy only some of these from open data, for example road width from OSM or HeiGIT.

Agency and news statements add a handful of names. Malé City Council closed **Majeedhee Magu between Dhanburuh Magu and Nikagas Magu** for flood works in December 2024. It reported repairs on **Chaandhanee Magu and Rahdhebai Magu**, said the areas near **IGMH and the STO flour warehouse** "experience severe flooding", and referred to its own survey of flood-prone areas ([Atoll Times, 28 Dec 2024](https://atolltimes.mv/post/news/10872)). In June 2024 it started with **Izzuddeen Magu, Maafannu** as "slow to drain", and the infrastructure minister called the **West Park area** "most impacted" ([Edition, 19 Jun 2024](https://edition.mv/jakarta/34122)). Temporary drains went in at West Park, the Villimalé ferry terminal and near IGMH in October 2024 ([Plus.mv, 13 Oct 2024](https://www.plus.mv/?p=104849)). MNDF pumps on 11 Jan 2025 were reported at West Park (5), near IGMH (2) and Boduvalhu (1) of nine areas ([The Standard, 11 Jan 2025](https://standard.mv/heavy-rainfall-causes-severe-flooding-in-male-mndf-mobilizes-to-drain-male-streets/)). **Majeedhee and Ameenee Magu** were named in an 18 Nov 2024 downpour when "nearly all streets" flooded ([Edition, 18 Nov 2024](https://edition.mv/un/37549)). In July 2026 the new **Boduthakurufaanu Magu section from Henveiru Ferry Terminal to Izzuddin Jetty** ponded because its pump station was not yet commissioned ([The Standard, 2026](https://standard.mv/fact-check-boduthakurufaanu-magu-flooding-was-not-caused-by-road-defects-or-a-design-flaw/)). Four days earlier the same section had flooded from a **tidal surge** ([Atoll Times, 26 Jul 2026](https://atolltimes.mv/post/news/16641)). Flood mechanism therefore has to be coded per event, not per road. The 2015 Maldives Independent feature adds Maafannu streets, Ameenee Magu and the Carnival area ([Maldives Independent, 17 Aug 2015](https://maldivesindependent.com/feature-comment/male-under-water-frequent-floods-demonstrate-climate-vulnerability-116499)).

At area level, the Council's 2025 VLR lists **8 major surface-water events in 2019–2024** and "frequent drainage failures in **Galolhu and Henveiru**". It separately records a rogue wave near Sinamalé Bridge and overtopping at Industrial Village ([MCC VLR 2025, pp. 92–93](https://cdn.malecity.gov.mv/website/documents/6zqnEssfa8YfgaNZkf_I0-VLR_Male_Small%20file%20size.pdf)). Its rainfall figures (179.7 mm on 31 Dec 2023; 143.4 mm on 12 Jan 2024) give no station or accumulation period, so record the period as unknown. The ward-level claim partly conflicts with a pure "south-west is wettest" reading, because Henveiru is in the east. The team should present both as hypotheses, not facts.

Equally telling is what the record lacks. Event reports for 13 Aug 2024 and 3–4 Jan 2025 name **no streets at all** ([Atoll Times, 13 Aug 2024](https://atolltimes.mv/post/news/9494); [Atoll Times, 4 Jan 2025](https://atolltimes.mv/post/news/10922)). The named places are main arterials, hospitals and ferry terminals, where reporters and pumps go. No Copernicus EMS, UNOSAT, Sentinel Asia or Charter activation covers Malé, which is expected for street ponding on a 2 km² island ([Charter activations](https://disasterscharter.org/fr/activations/flood-in-thailand-activation-1004-)). Mapillary, KartaView, OSM flood tags, social media and Dhivehi-language outlets (Mihaaru, Avas, Sun, PSM) were **not searched**. These are the most likely places to find more street names.

## Sub-daily rain is available only at Hulhulé or 10 km scale

No open hourly or sub-hourly gauge record exists for Malé island proper. The best open gauge route is the raw SYNOP archive for **Velana/Hulhulé (WMO 43555) on Ogimet**, which needs no login. Live checks returned reports from Dec 2021 to Oct 2026 carrying **6-hour and 12-hour** rain groups and a **24-hour** total, with some reports missing (NIL) ([Ogimet getsynop sample](https://www.ogimet.com/cgi-bin/getsynop?block=43555&begin=202405010000&end=202405011200)). No hourly rain group was seen, which matches the project's NOAA files. Ogimet's terms of use were not checked. Meteostat lists the same station with "hourly" precipitation that is **fully populated in 2023** even though the station reports 6-hourly. That pattern suggests redistribution or model filling, so it should not be treated as measured hourly rain. Its bulk feed also stops on 10 Mar 2026 ([Meteostat stations](https://bulk.meteostat.net/v2/stations/lite.json.gz)). The Maldives Meteorological Service publishes monthly climate reports but offers no public hourly or daily download ([MMS downloads](https://meteorology.gov.mv/downloads/199/view)).

Satellite rain fills in intensity but not location. **GPM IMERG V07** half-hourly data at 0.1° is public domain and needs a free Earthdata login. As of 15 Nov 2026 the research-grade **Final run ends on 30 Sep 2025**, about 13 months behind rather than the advertised ~4 months. The Late run covers the whole Dec 2021–Oct 2026 window but changes from **V07B to V07C between 1 and 10 March 2026** ([CMR Final](https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/granules.json?short_name=GPM_3IMERGHH&version=07&sort_key=-start_date&page_size=1); [CMR Late](https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/granules.json?short_name=GPM_3IMERGHHL&version=07&sort_key=-start_date&page_size=1)). One IMERG cell is about 120 km², compared with Malé's 1.95 km², and is mostly ocean. Studies in the Maritime Continent find IMERG reasonable for the top 5% of daily rain but **overestimating the top 1%** ([Reading CentAUR](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/id/eprint/101615)). The researchers took that finding from summaries and did not read the full text. JAXA's **GSMaP** is an hourly 0.1° alternative that requires registration and acknowledgement ([JAXA GSMaP](https://sharaku.eorc.jaxa.jp/GSMaP/guide.html)). ERA5 is reanalysis and CHIRPS is daily or dekadal, so neither is a sub-daily observation ([HDX CHIRPS dekadal](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/mdv-rainfall-subnational)). A non-peer-reviewed preprint reports Malé IDF curves from 1993–2024 hourly data, which implies MMS holds an hourly series that could be requested ([Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10674253/v1)).

For a road-susceptibility study, rainfall is an **event covariate**, not a spatial predictor. One 10 km cell or one Hulhulé gauge gives every Malé road the same value. Rainfall becomes useful only to describe each reported event (for example 12 h total at Hulhulé, IMERG peak 1 h), with the station, product version, UTC time stamps and accumulation window recorded.

## Open GIS layers cover roads and buildings, not drains or wards

The open base layers that resolve Malé island proper come from OSM and modelled population. The HOT/OSM country exports on HDX (roads, buildings, waterways; ODbL; last snapshot 3 Nov 2026) are the simplest refreshed road source ([HDX roads](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/hotosm_mdv_roads)). HDX also lists HeiGIT **road surface, width and passability** datasets, which could proxy JICA's "narrow roads without side drains", along with Meta and Kontur population layers. Their licences and their resolution over Malé were not checked one by one ([HDX search](https://data.humdata.org/api/3/action/package_search?q=maldives&rows=120)). Boundaries stop at island level. The COD-AB admin-2 layer (CC BY-IGO) contains "Male" but **no ward names** ([HDX cod-ab-mdv](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/cod-ab-mdv)), and geoBoundaries is no finer than atoll or city level ([geoBoundaries API](https://www.geoboundaries.org/api/current/gbOpen/MDV/ALL/)). The Bureau of Statistics lists Census 2022 "Block Maps" including Malé, but no downloadable GIS file appeared ([MBS Block Maps](https://statisticsmaldives.gov.mv/block-maps-of-maldives/)). Census "Maale" also includes Hulhumalé and Vilimalé, so its totals cannot be applied to the island proper ([MBS Census 2022](https://statisticsmaldives.gov.mv/census-2022/)). data.gov.mv is behind a Cloudflare challenge and must be browsed by hand. `lsa.gov.mv` and `geoportal.gov.mv` do not resolve. No open drain, inlet, outfall or pump-station layer was found anywhere, and pump stations should be checked in OSM (`man_made=pumping_station`) before being requested.

## Conclusion

Open data gives the team enough to ask a sharper question but not enough to answer it alone. "Which Malé roads are prone to flooding?" can be answered openly only as "which roads are **repeatedly reported**, and which open terrain and road features are associated with those reports". That is a ranking under heavy reporting bias, and every unreported road stays unknown. The honest design is therefore explainable and positive-unlabelled in spirit. The model should be built on OSM road attributes, coarse FABDEM-derived position, distance to known pump or intervention sites, and the qualitative south-west/reclaimed-land prior. It should say plainly that **no open evaluation set can measure false-alarm rates** at unseen streets.

The leverage lies in one or two agency documents rather than in more downloading. The government paid about US$320,000 for a Malé stormwater design that the World Bank's own report calls both delivered and not delivered, and the Council has said it surveyed flood-prone areas. If either reaches the team, the study can shift from mapping where people reported water to explaining why particular roads collect it. That outcome is worth framing as the project's stated next step in the six-page review.
