The gridded data underestimates the big days. On 3 Jan 2025 the Met Office measured 142 mm in Malé; the reanalysis shows 56 mm. On 20 Dec 2024 it was 136.5 mm against 25 mm. Reanalysis spreads a cloudburst over a 25 km cell, so it is good for timing and season but not for the intensity that drives street flooding. A working model needs the Met Service's own gauge readings, ideally hourly or finer.

What separates flood days

  • Median reanalysis rain on the 22 flood-labelled days: 34 mm. On all other days: 1 mm.
  • Median rain in the three days before: 27 mm against 6.5 mm. Wet ground and a high water table matter.
  • Median peak gust: 41 km/h against 32 km/h.
  • Daily minimum pressure barely differs (1008.3 against 1008.6 hPa), so pressure alone is a weak signal this close to the equator.

What a daily threshold misses

  • 41 days reached 40 mm in the reanalysis. Only 8 of them have a flood report.
  • Three flood days show under 20 mm. One (1 Apr 2023) shows under 1 mm: a local night storm the grid did not see.
  • 1 Mar 2025 flooded the city in about two hours. Intensity over one to three hours matters more than the daily total.
  • December and January produce as many incidents as the south-west monsoon, during the change between monsoons.

Flood labels

News reports are the only public record of where water stood. For a model, ask NDMA for its assistance-request logs (it counted 774 requests on 3 Jan 2025) and MNDF for pump deployment logs. Both carry addresses and times.

Official warnings

The Met Service publishes alerts as a machine-readable feed at cap.meteorology.gov.mv/rss/alerts/. White, yellow, orange and red levels make a ready baseline to beat.

Station rainfall caveat

The free NOAA copy of the Hulhulé record is sound for temperature, pressure and wind but its rainfall is incomplete: it totals under half the true annual rain and shows zero on several flood days. It also stops on 24 Aug 2025.
